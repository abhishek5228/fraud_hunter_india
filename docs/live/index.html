File format

Any common CSV works: column names are matched automatically (sender_id, amount_inr, timestamp, fraud_flag ...) and a note lists what was guessed. The canonical names are:
Required: ts, uid, amt, chan, mid and a location: city or lat, lon.
ts is epoch seconds or ISO-8601 (no zone means IST). chan is upi, ecom, pos, atm or imps. mid is the payee or merchant.
Optional: cat, dev, ip, pers, coll, rat, sim, and label: 0 good, 1 fraud the customer did not make, 2 fraud the customer was tricked into. With labels you get catch rates and money saved.
History matters. Give at least a month: the engine judges each payment against that customer's past, so a short file overstates friction.
Run the sample to see the report.

The engine attacks itself

An automated fraudster tries thousands of strategies against copies of real customers' history: how many payments, how much, to whom, from which device and network, at what hour, whether the customer is tricked into approving a challenge. The production engine decides every payment. Whatever slips through is the engine's blind spot.

What this does not prove

The attacker only has the levers listed in the table: a fixed menu, not a creative human. It attacks a simulator, and counts a challenge as letting through 8% of ordinary fraud and 50% of fraud the customer is tricked into. A clean result means these holes are closed, not that none remain.

How to read this tab

Everything on this tab uses real public data, not the simulator. Files are fetched from their original public sources onto the server, or you can upload your own copy. Nothing is stored in the repository. The card-fraud data has no customer, device or payee ids (they were anonymised), so it tests the model, not the behavioural engine. Full caveats: docs/BENCHMARKS.md.

What this can and cannot do

It reads wording and structure against known Indian scam patterns (OTP and PIN requests, screen-sharing apps, electricity-bill threats, fake KYC, 'digital arrest', look-alike payment handles, bad links). On the live server it also asks the risk engine whether the payee has many first-time senders or fraud complaints. It cannot tell who owns a payment handle, so it never says "safe".
